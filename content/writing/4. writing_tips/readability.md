Seven tips to improve readability?

Here's everything your need to know.

The most important thing is getting your copy *read*&mdash;and each and every element of your ad serve should serve this single purpose.

The following checklist will help to make your copy more *engaging*. To keep their eyes moving down the page.

Because it's the reading of your copy that gets you into your reader's head. They'll remember the stories, your product, your positioning... All those things you want them to remember and think about... *But only if they read your copy*.

Here are a few suggestions for getting them to do just that.

### 1. Write Short Intros

 Reading requires inertia. To suck your reader into your copy, open your piece with a short first sentences. It helps pull them in and get their eye moving across the page.

Something like:

* Secret's out.
* Blackwell blew it.
* This may surprise you.

Come up with a sentence that's seven words or less to start your copy. For a list of short sentence ideas, go HERE.

### 2. Keep it Snappy

An interesting rhythm keeps your reader moving through the text. Experiment with the following to give your copy a lively, swingin' beat.

* One sentence, one point
* Fragments = okay
* Hyphenate sentences&mdash;makes 'em shorter
* Begin sentences with "And," "But," "Because," "Or," "So"
* Search your document for "that." It can often be reworked or removed completely
* Alternate short and longer sentences
* Use transition words (for example, typically, as such, etc.)

Note that the above points (except for transitions) all serve brevity&mdash;sharing the greatest amount of emotion with the fewest words possible.

### 3. Simplify your Copy

If you want your copy to convert, people have to *understand it*. And make no mistake, readers go with the offer they most quickly understand.

The main point is simple words are more common, more easily recognized, and therefore, more persuasive.

* Leave the fancy shit at the door. Hammer out the main bits and do it clearly.
* Use plain, simple language. You didn't compose the piece, you wrote it. Your ""objective" is your goal. Obtain it?
* Stick with 1- and 2-syllable words (unless you're selling refridgerators)

Go through your copy and ask if a child could understand it. If not, adjust accordingly.

### 4. Chop Up Paragraphs

When writing for digital mediums (emails, ads, articles, etc.), I find a maximum of three sentences per paragraph works well. It makes your copy more scannable. People like that.

Search your copy for paragraphs longer than three sentences. When you find one, see if you can break it up into two (or more).

Writekickasscopy.com follows this rule.

### 5. Parse with Subheadings

Like short paragraphs, subheadings make your content more bite-sized and inviting to the reader.

Search your content for long streams of text and logical places for subheadings.

Here are a few approaches to subheadings:

* SEO: Introducing relevant keywords and topics
* AEO: Using user-asked questions as content subheadings
* Readability: Writing nonsense subheads to break up text visually

Your subheading approach will depend on your goals. But know this. The most valualbe use of subheadings is to break up text and make your copy more appealing to the reader.

### 6. Bulletpoint Written Lists

Search your copy for long sentences containing lists, and, if appropriate, use bulletpoints for each of the items instead.

Here's why:

* Bullets are easy to skim
* They break up text
* And that's all good

### 7. Grease the Slide

The last thing you want is readers getting stuck. To suck readers through your copy like a Hoover vacuum, try the following some of the following.

* Make honest and believable statements&mdash;ones you know your reader will *not* challenge
* Tease upcoming content ("in the next section," "more on that later," "stay tuned for," etc.)
* End paragraphs with "cliffhangers"

>> *But almost immediately, something strange happened.*
>>
>> *You think that's weird? Check this out.*
>>
>> *He was not worried about any of that. But there were other things.*
>>
>> *But here's something you may not have known.*

For these cliffhangers, go through your copy and force curiosity in at the end of paragraphs. You don't have to keep them, but the practice will keep you aware of how you can add a bit of curiosity to the ends of paragraphs to keep your reader sliding along.
